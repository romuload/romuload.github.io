Business context & problem

Modernize the platform. Rethink the work.

Porto Seguro had just invested in Salesforce Financial Services Cloud to replace a 40-year-old legacy system. The goal: a modern, scalable experience for the insurance brands operated by the Porto group (Porto, Azul, Itaú and Mitsui), brought together through acquisitions and partnerships.

Azul was acquired in 2003; Itaú Auto e Residência comes from a partnership with Itaú Unibanco since 2009; and Porto runs Mitsui Sumitomo’s auto insurance under a strategic partnership.

The business challenge

Build a common, scalable experience without carrying the fragmentation of the legacy workflow into the new platform. The migration created an opportunity to revisit how information and decisions were connected.

The analyst’s challenge

Understanding a claim meant moving between notices, policies, documents, history and external tools. Analysts had to reconnect the information before deciding what to do next.

The detailed design shown here focuses on APP/DC: passenger personal accident and bodily injury claims, including internal handling and regulation by an external service provider.

Scattered context

Claim, policy, documents and history in separate places.

Manual repetition

Data copied into notes, local text files and payment flows.

Unclear ownership

Analysts checked whether a task belonged to Porto or to the external service provider.

Limited visibility

Document status and case progress required additional checks.

My contribution

Connect discovery, design and delivery.

As Senior Product Designer working through GFT, I connected discovery, journey mapping, content, interface design and handoff for the new claims workflow.

Scope

Translate observations into journey maps, priorities, content and Salesforce interface proposals.

Collaboration

Work with analysts, managers, technology and product teams to understand the operation and align priorities.

Design contribution

Connect recurring pain points to a clearer structure for queues, case context and guided analysis.

Design constraints

Work with Lightning components, the Financial Services Cloud data model and OmniScript. Support a shared foundation across Porto, Azul, Itaú and Mitsui without multiplying custom interfaces.

Tools: Figma, Miro, Salesforce component libraries, Kraft and AI-assisted synthesis.

Design direction

Keep the claim in context. Make the next action clear.

The direction was to combine operational visibility with a guided path through the claim, reducing the need to reconstruct context across separate tools.

The key design question: how could the experience help an analyst understand the case and move forward with the relevant information at hand?

Consolidation

Bring the relevant context together.

Guidance

Make the sequence and next action explicit.

Clarity

Use language and states people can act on.

The constraint shaping the direction

A shared Salesforce foundation needed to serve four brands. I worked within Lightning and Financial Services Cloud patterns, focusing the proposal on information structure, sequence and clarity.

Evidence
Analysts moved between screens, notes and external tools to rebuild a claim.
Interpretation
The experience asked people to remember and reconnect information.
Design decision
Organize the journey around the case and its next decision, rather than isolated tasks.

From observations to priorities

Shadowing exposed the switches between systems and the manual checks behind each decision. AI supported grouping the mapped pain points, while workshops with analysts, managers, technology and product teams helped align the problem space and MVP priorities.

Content design

Make complexity actionable.

Language that guides

Content design focused on clearer terms, actionable messages and consistent labels. Cloze testing was used to explore whether analysts understood the wording in context.

Evidence
The content work examined terms, labels and messages in the analyst’s workflow.
Interpretation
A label only helps when people understand it in the context of the task.
Design decision
Use consistent terms and actionable messages; explore comprehension with cloze testing.

Prototyping & constraints

Moving the screens was not enough.

The first version migrated to Salesforce revealed the real problem, and a native platform resource became the answer.

01

The first impression

The technical team’s first migration simply redistributed the legacy data across Salesforce tabs. When we showed it to analysts, the concern was immediate:

“We modernized the system, but now I have to learn everything again. Where did the fields I always look for go?”
02

The question

How could we customize the experience so analysts would stop hunting for fields, as they did in the legacy system?

03

The turning point

I mapped the fields analysts kept searching for and grouped them into four steps. Then I took the problem to the Salesforce specialists, and the answer was OmniScript: native to the platform, it kept Salesforce’s standard layout while the guided flow brought data from several tabs and sources into each step. That is how the guided analysis was born.

Evidence
In the first migrated version, legacy fields were spread across Salesforce tabs, and analysts asked where their information had gone.

Design decision
Group the fields analysts searched for into four steps and deliver them as a guided analysis with OmniScript, native to Salesforce.

Outcomes & reflections

A clearer workflow.
Measured by observation.

The design work connects research findings to a common Salesforce experience, with operational visibility, guided analysis and mobile continuity.

−60%

Analysis time (up to)

45–60 min per claim in the legacy system, about 25 min with the guided analysis.

−40%

Navigation steps

Before/after estimate, counting the steps to complete the same claim in both versions.

How these were measured: I observed five analysts completing claims in the legacy system, where gathering information across tabs took 45 to 60 minutes per claim. With the four-step guided analysis, the same work took about 25 minutes. These are observational estimates, not production analytics; I left the project before production data was available.

What the work delivers

The four-step guided analysis, which I designed and took through implementation while on the project. The rest of the proposal (dashboard, queues, documents, regulation and payments) became backlog for the GFT team to continue after I left.

What success needs to track

Analysis time, navigation effort, incomplete-document rework, SLA by stage and the clarity of communication with customers and brokers.

What I take forward

Treat the workflow as a service, not a collection of screens. Use platform constraints to focus the design. Bring engineering into discovery early, test language in context and document the reasoning behind each decision.

What still needs to be measured

The observed estimates point to a clear gain. Confirming it at scale needs production data over an agreed period: analysis time, rework from incomplete documents and SLA by stage.
