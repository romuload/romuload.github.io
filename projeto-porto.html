Analysts searched across tabs and tools.

A 40-year-old claims system was moving to Salesforce for four insurance brands. Analysts still rebuilt each case from scattered fields, documents and local notes.

I led shadowing and discovery with analysts, managers, product and technology teams. The March 2024 blueprint records 14 days, 111 participants and 382 pain points and business rules; these describe discovery, not product outcomes.

Three decisions behind the guided analysis.

Organise the work, not the tabs

In the first migration, analysts still searched for familiar fields. I mapped the information they used and organised it into four steps: notice, policy, summary and opinion.

Use the platform’s native flow

I brought the problem to Salesforce specialists. With OmniScript, we could bring data from several sources into each step while keeping the standard Salesforce layout.

Check the language in context

I rewrote labels and messages in plain language and checked them with cloze testing, so analysts could understand what each step required.

Four steps. No more hunting for fields.

From the dashboard to the analyst’s opinion, the OmniScript flow brings each piece of information to the step where it is needed.

Original delivery: the four-step analysis I designed and supported through implementation in 2024. Dashboard, queues, documents and payments remained a proposal for the team’s backlog when I left.

Before/after estimate, counting the steps to complete the same claim in both versions.

How these were measured: I observed five analysts completing claims in the legacy system, where gathering information across tabs took 45 to 60 minutes per claim. With the four-step guided analysis, the same work took about 25 minutes. These are observational estimates, not production analytics; I left the project before production data was available.

What the work delivers

The four-step guided analysis, which I designed and took through implementation while on the project. The rest of the proposal (dashboard, queues, documents, regulation and payments) became backlog for the GFT team to continue after I left.

What I would measure next

Grouping information around the analyst’s task mattered more than reproducing legacy screens. I would validate the observed time savings with production data, including rework and SLA by stage.
